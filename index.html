    Finished bags

    Three things in every cup

    Taste
    On the tongue. Sour, sweet, bitter, salty. There are only a handful.
    Mouthfeel
    In the whole mouth. Body from thin to heavy, texture from tea-like to syrupy, and dryness, which is a feeling rather than a taste.
    Aroma
    In the nose, including what rises from the back of your mouth. Everything with a name, like strawberry, jasmine or chocolate, lives here.
    Try it: pinch your nose and sip. What you get is taste. Let go, and what arrives is aroma.

    Three easy mix-ups

    Acidity or sour
    Acidity is the pleasant brightness of a good apple. Sour is sharp, thin and pinching, and usually means under extraction. Light roasts should be bright, not sour.
    Bitter or drying
    Bitter is a taste at the back of the tongue, like tonic water. Drying is a furry feeling on the gums, like stewed black tea. Over extraction shows up as drying more often than bitter.
    Strength or extraction
    Weak or strong is a separate dial from sour or bitter. A cup can be weak and bitter, or strong and sour. That is why the log asks them as two questions.

    How to taste a cup

    1. Taste it hot, warm and near room temperature. Acidity and sweetness grow as it cools, and so do faults.
    2. Slurp. It spreads the coffee and pushes aroma up to your nose.
    3. Acidity: is there any, and is it lemon sharp, apple crisp or grape juicy?
    4. Sweetness: none, some, or like fruit juice?
    5. Body: tea-like, silky or heavy?
    6. Finish: clean, or drying and lingering?
    7. Only then, what it reminds you of. Start broad on the flavour wheel and narrow down only if you are sure.

    Roaster notes are hints, not a test. If the bag says guava and you get sweet tropical fruit, you are tasting the same thing with fewer words.

    Your grinder: Timemore C5

    Timemore's own spec for the C5 series: 0.031 mm per click, 48 clicks per rotation, 16 macro steps, and a finest grind setting of 3. They say a 3 click buffer is the practical zero point, there to protect the burrs. So close the dial to the stop, count out, and treat 3 as the finest you should ever use.

    The C5 Pro matches the C5 on every number that affects grind, and the difference is the pull to fold handle, so settings transfer between them. The ESP models are genuinely different: 0.015 mm per click, 50 clicks per rotation, 10 macro steps. Their numbers do not transfer.

    How the estimates in this log were made

    Each recipe's own grind was converted to a burr gap, then divided by 31 µm. A Comandante steps about 30 µm from burr contact too, so a Comandante click count carries across almost one to one. Recipes that name only a style were placed on this scale: fine about 450 µm (14 clicks), medium fine about 600 µm (19), medium about 750 µm (24), medium coarse about 900 µm (29), coarse about 1100 µm (35).

    One anchor disagrees. The Coffee Chronicler grinds his Switch recipe at 22 to 24 Comandante clicks, but says his own C5 needed 15 to 17 for pour over, which is 5 to 7 clicks finer than this conversion. So if a recipe tastes thin and drains fast at the click number shown here, go finer rather than assuming the coffee is at fault.

    The buffer shifts everything. These numbers assume the burrs close at click 0. Timemore's 3 click buffer suggests contact actually sits near click 3, which would make every estimate here 3 clicks fine. That is the second reason to treat them as a starting point rather than a setting.

    Conversions from a 1Zpresso ZP6, a K-Ultra, a Kinu or a café EK43 are the least reliable, since their step sizes and zero points are not comparable. Those recipes are placed by description.

    Adjusting from the cup

    Sour, thin
    Grind 1 to 2 clicks finer, or brew 2 to 3 °C hotter. On the Switch, a longer steep also helps.
    Bitter, dry
    Grind 1 to 2 clicks coarser, or brew cooler. Check you are not pressing through the hiss.
    Weak
    Raise the dose or tighten the ratio before touching grind. For concentrate recipes, add less bypass water.
    Too strong
    Add bypass water after brewing, or loosen the ratio.
    Switch drains slowly
    Grind coarser. Fines and very fine settings stall the drawdown and push the cup bitter.

    Change one thing per brew and write it in "Change next time", so the log can tell you what worked.
